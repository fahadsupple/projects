What a smart toilet actually does

A smart toilet, sometimes called an intelligent toilet, an electric bidet or a Japanese toilet, replaces paper-and-flush with a set of electronic functions built into the pan or the seat. The features people talk about most are the practical ones. A heated seat, so the first contact on a cold Melbourne morning is not a shock. A warm-water bidet wash with an adjustable nozzle, which is the core of the whole category. A warm-air dryer that follows the wash. On top of those, most models add an auto open-and-close lid, a soft night light, and a self-cleaning or deodorising cycle.

Two body styles cover almost everything on the market. An integrated smart toilet suite is a single unit where the pan, cistern and electronics are designed together. A bidet seat is an electronic seat that bolts onto a compatible pan you may already own, which is the cheaper way in and a common first step. Knowing which of the two you want is the first real decision, and it shapes both the price and the install.

The power point every smart toilet needs

This is the detail that catches the most buyers out, and it is worth reading before you commit. A smart toilet is an electrical appliance. It needs a power point, a GPO, near the toilet, and in many bathrooms that means an electrician runs a new outlet as well as a plumber connecting the water. If your bathroom has no power near the pan, factor that work into the budget from the start.
