## What a smart toilet actually does

A smart toilet, sometimes called an intelligent toilet, an electric bidet or a Japanese toilet, replaces paper-and-flush with a set of electronic functions built into the pan or the seat. The features people talk about most are the practical ones. A heated seat, so the first contact on a cold Melbourne morning is not a shock. A warm-water bidet wash with an adjustable nozzle, which is the core of the whole category. A warm-air dryer that follows the wash. On top of those, most models add an auto open-and-close lid, a soft night light, and a self-cleaning or deodorising cycle.

Two body styles cover almost everything on the market. An integrated smart toilet suite is a single unit where the pan, cistern and electronics are designed together. A bidet seat is an electronic seat that bolts onto a compatible pan you may already own, which is the cheaper way in and a common first step. Knowing which of the two you want is the first real decision, and it shapes both the price and the install.

## The power point every smart toilet needs

This is the detail that catches the most buyers out, and it is worth reading before you commit. A smart toilet is an electrical appliance. It needs a power point, a GPO, near the toilet, and in many bathrooms that means an electrician runs a new outlet as well as a plumber connecting the water. If your bathroom has no power near the pan, factor that work into the budget from the start.

Two related points come up constantly. First, power outages: the heated seat, the wash and the dryer all rely on the power being on, while most smart toilets keep a manual or mechanical flush so the toilet still flushes when the power drops. Confirm the exact power-out behaviour of any specific model before you buy. Second, water: smart toilets should run on fresh mains water, not recycled greywater, and a WELS water-efficiency rating tells you how the suite uses water. ## What buyers want to know before choosing one

**Is a smart toilet worth it?** Opinions genuinely split. Some owners call it worth every cent for the daily comfort, while others decide the cost is not justified for their household. The wash, warm seat and dryer are what long-term owners rave about. If those matter to you, the value follows.

**How much do smart toilets cost?** Bidet-seat setups start near $1,500, integrated suites usually sit between about $2,000 and $4,500, and premium models run past $6,000. Skyflex confirms current U6 and U7 pricing on enquiry.

**What are the disadvantages?** The main ones are the upfront cost, the need for a power point and possibly an electrician, and the fact that the wash, dryer and heated seat depend on power. There are more electronic parts than a standard toilet, so servicing is a consideration over the years.

**How is a smart toilet installed?** It needs a plumber for the water connection and, in many bathrooms, an electrician to add a power point near the pan. If your bathroom has no outlet close by, factor that work into the budget from the start.

**Do smart toilets work when the power goes out?** The electronic functions stop, but most models retain a manual or mechanical flush so the toilet still works. Check the specific model's power-out behaviour before buying.

**Do you still use toilet paper?** You can, though many owners find the warm-water wash and air dryer reduce how much they reach for it. It is a preference, not a rule.

**Can I fit a bidet seat to my existing toilet, or do I need a whole new suite?** If your pan is compatible, a bidet seat is the lower-cost route and avoids replacing the whole suite. An integrated suite is the option when you want everything designed together or you are renovating anyway.

**Which toilets do plumbers prefer?** Plumbers tend to favour WELS-rated suites with standard connections and serviceable parts. The best guide is the person installing yours, so ask your plumber to sight the model and the bathroom before you order.
