## Four Things That Decide What Your Door Costs

You could buy the same smart lock twice and pay two different amounts to have it fitted. The lock is a fixed price on a shelf. The work around it is not, and four things account for most of the gap.

- What is already cut into the door. A door with a deadbolt in it, sized and positioned the way the new lock expects, is close to a straight swap. A door with only a handle set, or with nothing in it at all, has to be prepared first, and preparing takes longer than replacing.
- What the leaf is made of and how thick it is. Solid timber, a metal frame and a glazed door are three different jobs needing three different sets of tools. Thickness alone rules models in or out.
- Whose lock it is. Hardware you have already bought means the quote can cover the labour on its own.
- How many doors are on the list. Each one gets its own line, so a property with a front door, a garage door and a side gate gets three answers.

None of that reads down a phone line, so the figure comes after somebody has stood in front of the door. Three things make the quote accurate first time: what the door belongs to, what is on it now, and the model if you have settled on one.

## What Changes When the Door Belongs to a Business

If the door is on a business, it works harder than a house door and the hardware has to match. A domestic entry gets opened a handful of times a day by the same few people. A trade door gets opened constantly, on different shifts, often by people who will have moved on within the year.

Three things separate the two in practice.

- Heavier furniture. Trade doors often carry a closer, a wider frame, or a mortice lock reaching further into the edge than a domestic deadbolt does. Not every consumer smart lock is built to sit in one.
- More people through it. A household shares a couple of codes and leaves it at that. A business issues them and withdraws them, and wants that done quickly.
- More than one opening. An office door, a garage or roller door, a gate and a shed can all land on the same job, and they rarely want the same lock on each.

Leased premises add one step. The owner or managing agent usually wants to see what is going on the door before it goes on, and the quote is specific enough to hand over.

## Whether the Lock You Like Suits the Door You Have

Before anything is ordered, a locksmith reads your door: the lockset in it now, what the leaf is made of, how thick it is, and what has already been cut. Lockwood, Lane and Lemaar cover much of what is on Melbourne doors, and a smart deadbolt or smart mortice lock will often take over from one of them using preparation already there.

Where the door has never carried a deadbolt, the openings are made on the day: a bore through the face, a hole through the edge for the bolt, and a recess cut into the jamb for it to land in.

Fitting follows on the same visit. Once the lock is on, the door gets worked back and forth so the bolt drops into its recess cleanly and nobody has to lean on it. Codes go on after that, prints enrolled if the model reads them, the app paired if there is one. You use the lock yourself before the locksmith drives off.
