## What Comes Off the Door and What Stays On It

Your flat's entry probably carries two separate pieces of hardware: a handle set with a latch that holds the door shut, and above it a deadlock whose bolt passes through the face of the door into the frame. The two work independently.

A smart deadbolt is built for the deadlock's position, not the handle's. Where the opening your deadlock sits in suits the model you chose, the old lock lifts out, the new one goes in, and the handle set is never touched. That is the short version, and the common one on a unit door.

Where you would rather keep the deadlock working, it stays and a fresh position is marked out and prepared on the same leaf. The door then carries two locks, one mechanical and one electronic, and the visit runs longer because the door is cut rather than swapped. Replacing the whole door hardware, handle set and all, is a different job again, and rarely what a flat needs in order to gain keypad entry.

Which of those you are looking at comes down to the opening the deadlock already occupies, the thickness of the leaf, and whether it is timber or metal-framed. Most deadlocks on Melbourne entries carry a Lockwood, Lane or Lemaar name on the faceplate, and the new lock has to suit the opening whichever it is. That gets measured at the door with the lock off, since the fixings behind it are hidden while it is still screwed together.

## What Still Holds the Door Once It Opens With a Code

A smart deadbolt is still a deadbolt. The bolt is metal, it throws into a strike screwed into the frame, and that pairing is what physically holds the door closed. Electronics change who can operate the bolt and how, not what the bolt is made of.

So the mechanical side keeps the attention it always had. A strike that has worked loose, or a door that has dropped far enough that the bolt no longer lands square, is put right before the new lock goes on. Moving from a key to a code does not give that part away. It changes what unlocks the bolt, and adds codes you can issue and withdraw, a fingerprint on models that read one, or the app.

## Renting, Letting and Putting the Door Back

If you rent your flat, or you own it and let it out, the person choosing the lock is not always the person who owns the door. That changes one thing worth settling before a model is bought.

Where the smart lock uses the opening the deadlock already left, the change is reversible. A mechanical deadlock goes back into that same opening later and the door is as it was. Where a second position has been prepared instead, the door carries that preparation permanently. Neither is wrong, but on a door you do not own outright, knowing which one you are agreeing to is worth more than any feature list.

Codes also change what happens when the people using the door change: retiring one and issuing another takes no hardware at all, while a rekey needs the deadlock itself. A leased shop entry in Prahran works the same way, and the door still belongs to whoever owns the building.
