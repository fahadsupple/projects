## Where the Deadbolt Goes on a Door That Never Had One

If the only hardware on your front door is a handle with a latch, the new lock has to make its own place in the door. Nothing is sitting there waiting for it, and that is neither a barrier nor unusual. It means one decision comes before any tool does, and it is yours to make.

There are two ways it can land.

- The handle set stays, and a smart deadbolt goes in above it. The latch keeps holding the door shut as it always has, and the new lock adds a bolt that throws into the frame, opened by a code, a fingerprint or the app.
- The handle set comes out, and a smart lockset takes its place. Several models are a complete handle and lock in one unit, so the door keeps a single point of hardware and gains keyless entry at the height your hand already goes.

Two things about the door narrow the model list. Its thickness has to fall inside the range printed on the box. Its material sets how it is prepared, since a solid timber leaf and a metal-framed door are cut and fixed differently.

Marking out happens at your address on the day, and it does not get undone. Which is why the position is agreed with you rather than assumed from the packaging.

## Keeping a Deadbolt That Was Only Just Fitted

You may be at the other end of this, with a deadlock a locksmith fitted recently and no appetite for pulling out hardware that has barely been used. A Lockwood double cylinder deadlock a few months old is a starting point, not a mistake to correct.

The smart lock can go on as a second position on the same door, leaving the deadlock where it is. That means fresh preparation somewhere the existing hardware is not, and a door that carries both. Two locks on one entry earn their keep when both get thrown, or when the mechanical one is the fallback while batteries are on charge.

Or the deadlock comes out and the smart deadbolt goes into the opening it leaves. That is the shorter of the two visits, because the bore, the backset and the strike already exist and only have to suit the new model. The lock that comes off stays yours, and a deadlock in good order has life left on a rear entry, a garage door or a shed.

## Handle Sets, New Deadlocks and Fit-Only Jobs

### My front door has never had a deadbolt. Does that rule out a smart lock?

No. It changes what the visit involves rather than whether it can happen. Name the model and say whether the door is timber or metal-framed, and the answer comes back before you commit to a time.

### Can the smart lock go above the handle, or does the handle have to come out?

Either, and the model decides it more than the door does. A smart deadbolt is built to sit above a handle set and leave it alone, so the latch keeps behaving as it does now. A full smart lockset replaces the handle, and one piece of hardware then does both jobs.

### The deadlock in my door is only a few months old. Does it have to come out?

Not unless you want it to. A recent Lockwood or Lane deadlock can stay where it is, with the smart lock prepared at a separate position on the same door. The alternative is to take it out and put the smart deadbolt into the opening it leaves.

### I can handle the app and the codes myself. Can I book the fitting only?

Yes, and mention it when you enquire. The standard finish is the lock left working with your codes on it and the app paired. Where you would rather do the setup later, the visit ends once the bolt is finding its strike cleanly.
