## What Is Already Cut Into the Door

Two shapes account for most entry doors. A round hole bored through the face of the door, close to the edge, carries a deadbolt. A rectangular pocket routed into the edge itself carries a mortice lock. Which one you have decides the direction: a smart deadbolt wants the first, a smart mortice lock takes over the second.

Then it comes down to whether that shape sits in the right place, and at the right size, for the lock you have chosen. The door also has to be the thickness that lock expects. Older hardware was cut to its own dimensions, so on a door from a 1960s block the opening that is there and the lock you have chosen do not always agree. Adjusting it to suit is ordinary work when it is planned into the job.

The brand stamped on the current lock, commonly Lockwood, Lane or Lemaar on Melbourne doors, tells you less than the shape it left behind. All three make both types.

Where nothing is cut into the door yet, it gets marked out and prepared on site, which takes longer than a swap and is standard work. Mention whether the door is timber or metal-framed when you enquire, because the two are prepared differently. That reading of the door is the first part of the visit, and fitting only follows once the lock and the door are confirmed to suit each other.

## If Your Entry Door Is Fire-Rated

A fire-rated door is built and tested as a complete assembly, hardware included, so what goes into one has to be made for that door. Smart locks are made in fire-rated versions for unit doors, and a Yale Unity entrance lock comes in one. That is a compatibility question about the model, settled at your door rather than assumed from the packaging.

Confirming whether yours is fire-rated comes first. If the building's paperwork mentions it, or the door edge carries a tag, say so when you enquire and the model can be narrowed before you spend anything. Otherwise it is one of the things read on the day, before any hardware is ordered or the old lock comes off.

Yes, provided the lock itself is made for a fire-rated door. Those versions exist across several ranges, a fire-rated Yale Unity entrance lock among them. The important part is choosing the model with the rating already known, which is why the door is checked before anything is ordered.
