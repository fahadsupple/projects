## Whether Your New Lock Uses the Hole That Is There

Start at the face of your door and move inwards. A deadbolt sits in two holes: one bored through the face near the edge for the body of the lock, one in from the edge for the bolt. A smart deadbolt wants the same pair, which is why a straight swap is possible at all.

Four things decide whether yours is a swap or a fresh preparation:

- The size of the hole through the face, against what the new lock is built for.
- The backset, meaning the distance from the door edge in to the centre of that hole. Older Australian hardware and current smart locks do not always share one, though many locks arrive with an adjustable latch.
- The thickness of the leaf, against the range printed on the box.
- Where the bolt lands in the frame, and whether the strike plate suits the new one.

Get all four and the old lock lifts out and the new one goes in. Miss one and the door is prepared to suit first, which is longer rather than harder. How it is prepared then depends on the leaf itself, since a solid timber door and a metal-framed one are cut and fixed differently.

## The Three Old Locks That Turn Up on These Doors

What is on your door now is likely to be one of three things. Hardware of that generation was built to last, which is often why nobody has touched the door since.

- A deadbolt through the face, with a round plate on each side and a keyway outside. This is the one a smart deadbolt takes over most directly.
- A mortice lock in the edge of the door, sitting in a long pocket with a plate running along the edge. A smart mortice lock uses that same pocket.
- A deadlatch mounted on the inside face, with only a cylinder outside. It reads like a deadbolt from where you stand with the key, and a smart lock built as a through-door assembly needs a different opening from it.

Lockwood, Lane and Lemaar all made versions of these, and a Gainsborough-style set puts the lock inside the knob. Name yours when you enquire and the shortlist starts narrowing before anyone arrives.

## Bores, Backsets and Swapping Out an Old Set

### Our entry has a Gainsborough-style knob set. Can a smart lock take its place?

Yes, in one of two ways. A complete smart lockset replaces the knob set and uses the opening it leaves, so the door carries one piece of hardware. Or the knob set stays and a smart deadbolt goes in above it. Which you pick is a preference, not a technical limit.

### The deadbolt in the door is still sound. Does it have to come out?

Only if the smart lock is going where it sits. Where you would rather keep it, the new lock is prepared at a separate position on the same door and the old bolt carries on. Plenty of entries end up with both, one mechanical and one electronic.

### The old lock is a brand I have never heard of. Does that matter?

Not for whether a smart lock can go on. It matters where a set is old enough that parts are no longer made: servicing stops being an option and replacement is the sensible answer. Tell us what is stamped on it and you will hear which side of that line yours sits on.

### Will the smart lock answer to the same app as everything else in the house?

That depends on the model rather than the installation. Some run their own app only, and others join a wider home platform alongside cameras and doorbells. If you already run one, check the lock supports it before you buy: that is set by the hardware, not by anything done on the day.

### Does older hardware make the job dearer than a straight swap?

Not on its own. What adds to a quote is the preparation the door needs: enlarging an opening, moving it, or making good around it. An old set sitting in an opening the new lock already suits is a straight swap like any other.
