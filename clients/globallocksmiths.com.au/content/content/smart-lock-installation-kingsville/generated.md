## Which Doors Your Old Key Still Opens

Whether your existing key still opens the front door afterwards comes down to one feature on the model you buy: a mechanical key cylinder. Three arrangements come out of that, and they feel different day to day.

- **Your key, and a code on top.** The new lock keeps a cylinder, cut to the key you already use. The front door opens either way, and the back door, laundry and garage are untouched.
- **A code at the front, your key everywhere else.** The model has no cylinder, so the front door leaves your key set and runs on a code, a fingerprint or the app.
- **One new key, and a code at the front.** Everything staying mechanical is rekeyed on the same visit, so the doors left on metal answer to one key rather than the two or three collected over the years.

The third suits anyone who has just taken a property over and wants a clean start on the keys.

## When the Keys Are on a Register

If your keys can only be cut on somebody's say-so, the property runs a restricted master key system. Keys are issued against a register, so the site knows how many exist and who holds each one. That control is worth protecting when one door goes electronic.

A smart lock can join a register or sit outside it. Where the model keeps a cylinder, that cylinder is supplied to suit the restricted system and the door stays on the master key with everything else. Where it has none, the door keeps its own list of codes, prints and app users, and whoever holds the register needs to know that beforehand.

A single smart lock is not an access control system: it holds its credentials on the door itself, which is fine for one or two openings. Access control administers users centrally, with time zone settings, audit trails and the ability to add or remove someone quickly, and we install those too. Past a couple of doors, ask about both.

## Which Models Your Door Leaves in the Running

Look at the face of the door, then at its edge. A hole bored through the face carries a deadbolt, and a smart deadbolt takes its place when the hole size, the distance in from the edge and the leaf thickness suit the model. ## Old Keys, Old Doors and What It Costs

### Can you put a smart lock on an old door?

Usually, yes: what matters is what is cut into it rather than its age. Older entries often carry a mortice lock in the edge, and a smart mortice lock replaces it in the same pocket. A door that has dropped or swelled may need its strike adjusted so it latches cleanly, and that is part of the job.

### Will the key I have now still open the front door once the smart lock is on?

Only where the model has a mechanical cylinder in it and we cut that cylinder to your key. Say at the enquiry that you want the existing key to survive, and the shortlist narrows to models that allow it.

### Our site runs a restricted key system. Can one door go electronic without leaving the register?

It can, on a model with a cylinder we supply to suit the restricted system: the door stays on the master key while gaining a keypad or reader. A model without one runs its own credential list instead, which is a decision for whoever holds the register.

### If we rekey the other doors, what happens to the keys already handed out?

They stop opening the rekeyed doors, and you issue the new key to whoever should have it: one key, one list.
