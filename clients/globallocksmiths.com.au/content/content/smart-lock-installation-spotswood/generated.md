## Which Holes Are New and Which Were Already There

Start with the hardware on your door now. A deadbolt sitting above the handle means two openings already exist: a round bore through the face of the door, and a smaller hole through the edge for the bolt to travel along. A smart deadbolt is built around exactly that pair, so it goes in where the old one comes out and nothing fresh is cut in the leaf.

Three things are still new on a swap like that, and none is a hole through the face:

- Screw holes for the new plates. They bite into clean timber and sit under the hardware once it is on.
- A recess in the door frame for the strike, where the new bolt lands differently from the old one. That is the frame, not the door.
- Nothing else through the leaf. On models built in two halves, the cable joining them runs through the bore that is already there.

Where the edge of your door carries a rectangular mortice lock instead, a smart mortice lock takes over the pocket routed for it. Lockwood, Lane and Lemaar all make both types, so a like-for-like retrofit is usually available for whatever is on the door now.

The other case is a door that has only ever had a handle set on it. Then a bore and an edge hole are made, sized to the model you chose. That is genuine new preparation, and it is the part that cannot be taken back, so it is agreed with you before anything is cut.

## If You Ever Take the Smart Lock Off Again

Nobody buys a lock planning to remove it. It is still worth knowing what would be left in the door if you did.

A bore through the face and a hole through the edge are the standard preparation a keyed deadbolt is built around too. So a door that has carried a smart lock takes an ordinary mechanical deadbolt straight back, as long as the replacement suits the same bore and sits the same distance in from the edge. That is a lock swap rather than a repair.

Two smaller things decide how tidy that looks. A smart lock's outside plate is often wider than a mechanical rose, so a later lock may not cover every screw hole; those fill and the face is made good. And where a second position was cut so an existing deadlock could stay, that preparation is in the leaf for good, which is the one part worth deciding slowly.

The frame is easier again. A strike recess is simply covered by the next strike that goes into it.

## Drilling, Wiring and Taking It Off Again

### Does fitting a smart lock always mean drilling the front door?

No. Where a deadbolt is already in the door, the new lock uses the openings it leaves. A door that has only ever carried a handle set has a bore and an edge hole made for it, and which of those you have is read at your door before anything is ordered.

### Which smart locks need the least cutting?

The ones sold as retrofits for the hardware you already have. A smart deadbolt for an existing bore, or a smart mortice lock for an existing mortice pocket, both work with the preparation in the door instead of adding to it.

### Could I drill the door myself and have you fit the lock?

Marking out and drilling are part of the fitting itself. Positions come off the lock's own template with the door hanging in its frame, so the bolt meets the strike squarely. A suitable hole already in the door is simply less to do on the day.

### We might sell one day. Can a normal keyed lock go back on afterwards?

Yes. A mechanical deadbolt is built around the same bore and edge hole, so one goes back where the other came out. The plate is the thing to watch: where the smart lock's is the wider, the screw holes outside the new one are filled and the face made good.
