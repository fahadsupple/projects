## Keeping the Cylinder You Already Have

You have three possible outcomes, and the one you get is decided before anything is bought.

The first leaves the cylinder where it is. Plenty of entry doors already carry two locks, a mortice lock in the edge and a deadbolt through the face, so the smart lock takes one position and the other is left alone. Your key keeps turning in the lock it always turned in, and you gain a second way of opening the door: a code, a fingerprint or the app.

The second moves the cylinder across. Some lock bodies accept a separate cylinder rather than arriving sealed with their own. Where the two agree on type, the cylinder in your door goes into the new lock and every key cut to it carries on working. That is what people mean when they say the existing oval cylinder has to stay.

The third replaces the lot. The old cylinder comes out and the smart lock arrives with its own, or with no keyway at all. Keys cut to the old one stop opening that door the day the work is done: right at a changeover, wrong if other people still need to get in.
