## Not Every Door Takes a Smart Deadbolt

Look at the door before you look at the lock. Smart lock hardware is built around a door with a solid edge, a stile wide enough to carry a lock body, and a frame it shuts against. A hinged front door gives you all three. A sliding door does not, and a panel of glass does not either.

Around Maidstone all three keep turning up: a screen door sitting over a slider, a glazed patio door someone wanted made lockable, a straight smart deadbolt going onto a hinged entry. No one type is the default here.

- A hinged entry with a lock already in it. This is what a smart deadbolt or a smart mortice lock is built for, and it is usually the shortest visit.
- A sliding door. Sliders take their own hardware, and not every smart lock range includes a version for one. Whatever goes on has to suit the track and the way the door closes.
- A door that is mostly glass. There is no stile to carry a deadbolt through, so anything fitted works off the frame rather than the leaf, and sometimes the honest answer is that your chosen model is not the one for that opening.

None of the three rules out smart entry. They rule in different hardware.

## When a Screen Door Sits in Front of the Main One

Where it sits close, the depth of the new lock body and the position of a keypad decide which model goes on. Two locks on one opening should work as one easy sequence, not a puzzle you solve with shopping in the other hand. The screen belongs in the quote.

## If Your Door Already Has a Deadbolt

Then you are in the simplest version of this. Three measurements decide it: how wide the existing hole is, how far its centre sits from the edge of the door, and how thick the door is. If the lock you chose is built for those, it goes in where the old one was.

Where the entry has a mortice lock instead, the smart equivalent uses the pocket already routed into the door's edge. Whether the current hardware is a Lockwood, a Lane or a Lemaar matters less than which of the two types it is, since all three make both.

A door with no hole in it at all is a longer afternoon: marked out, cut and prepared to take the lock, and entirely routine work. Timber and metal-framed doors are prepared differently, so the material is confirmed before anyone books a time.

## What to Check Before You Buy the Lock

Already bought one? That is fine. A lock you supplied gets fitted exactly as one we supply would, and the make and model is enough to answer the fit question before anyone books a time.

If you have not bought yet, four things narrow it down fast.

- The door type first. A range built for hinged timber entries does not always include a slider or a glazed-frame version, and that is the check the packaging is quietest about.
- A mechanical key cylinder, if you want one. Decide it at the checkout, because no cylinder can be retrofitted into a lock built without one.
- The door's thickness, and whether there is a hole in it already.
- Clearance behind the screen door, where there is one. It decides whether a keypad on the inner door can be reached comfortably.

## Questions About Fit, Cost and Doing It Yourself

### Can a smart lock go on a sliding door?

Some can, though not by dropping a deadbolt into it, because there is nothing to drop one into. A slider locks at the stile or the frame, and that hardware is a separate product line from the one built for a hinged door. Establish whether the range you like makes a slider version at all.

### What if the lock I have already bought turns out not to fit?

You find out before a date is booked and before anything is cut, which is the whole point of asking first. From there we name what does suit that door, and you decide whether to return the one you have or keep it for another opening.

### Can I install a smart door lock myself?

It depends entirely on which door. A hinged entry whose deadbolt already sits where the new lock wants it is a swap, and plenty of people do it over a weekend. A slider or a glazed door is not a fitting problem at all, because the wrong device does not become the right one with more care. Where a hole has to be cut for the first time, that cut stays cut.

### Do smart locks void insurance?

Your insurer sets that, and policies differ enough that it is worth a call to yours before you commit to a model. What we can give you is a clear record of what went on the door: the exact lock fitted, by a locksmith licensed and insured in Victoria.
