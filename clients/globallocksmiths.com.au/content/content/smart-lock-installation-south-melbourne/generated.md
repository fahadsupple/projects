## The One Door in the Flat That Is Not Only Yours

Your entry door hangs on the boundary between your flat and the part of the building everybody shares. That is why it is treated differently from every door behind it. Hardware on that boundary is typically the building's to approve rather than the occupant's, and in a strata building it is the owners corporation that does the approving.

It catches people out, because the door does not feel shared. You lock it, you carry the key, your name sits beside it. The leaf you are asking to drill is also the one the corridor sees, and that is where the rule comes from.

Not every South Melbourne job has a building in it. Plenty are plain timber front doors on houses, where the only approval needed is your own and the whole question collapses to whether the lock suits the door.

## Where the Answer Comes From, and What to Ask For

The building's own records say what your entry door is classed as, and whoever manages the building can read them. If you rent, the question goes to your managing agent rather than straight to the corporation.

Two things are worth putting in the same message:

- Whether hardware on your entry door may be changed, and on what conditions.
- Whether the building already has an installer appointed for its doors. Some do, and it is a one-line question rather than something to assume either way.

Ask in writing. An answer you can point to later is worth more than one remembered from a conversation in the lobby, and it costs nothing but the typing.

You will probably be asked in return who would be doing the work, and whether they are licensed and insured. That answer is short.

## The Lock in the Box, and the Door It Has to Suit

Your door almost certainly carries a lock already, and the new one goes into the space that leaves. Two versions of that retrofit turn up on South Melbourne entries.

Where a deadbolt is already bored through the face of the door, a smart deadbolt goes back into that bore, provided the diameter, the backset and the leaf thickness suit the model you picked. Where the door carries a mortice lock in its edge instead, a smart mortice lock takes over the same pocket. Much of the mechanical hardware on Melbourne entries carries a Lockwood, Lane or Lemaar name, and the new lock has to mate with whichever is on yours.

Where nothing suitable is cut, the door is prepared on site: marking out, drilling through the face, and chisel work on the edge to suit the exact model. That is a longer visit than a swap, and entirely ordinary. It is also the part that cannot be undone, which is the reason the building question belongs first.

If the lock is already bought, book it in as it stands. A lock you supplied is fitted to the same standard as one we supply. Send the make and model with your enquiry and the fit is confirmed before a date goes in the diary.

If nothing is ordered yet, the door narrows the field before any feature does: thickness, what is already cut into it, and whether the entry is fire-rated.
