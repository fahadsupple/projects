## Who Has to Say Yes Before the Lock Goes On

Whose sign-off you need comes down to who controls the door.

- You own the house and the door is yours alone. The decision is yours, and the only question left is which lock suits it.
- You own an apartment or a unit. Hardware on a common-property or common-entrance door is controlled by the owners corporation, so that is where the authorisation comes from.
- You rent through an agent. The managing agent signs off hardware changes on the owner's behalf.
- The door belongs to a business or a health-care site. Whoever holds the key register needs to agree before anything on it changes.

An owners corporation or an agent is deciding three things: which lock is proposed, what preparing the door involves, and whether the door still closes and latches the way it does now. All three sit on a single quote.

## Doors That Have to Keep Closing and Latching

Your door may be doing a second job. A unit entry or a corridor door with a closer on it has to swing shut and latch by itself every time. An exit door also carries fire-safety obligations that limit what may be substituted onto it. That narrows the choice rather than closing it off, and fire-rated smart locks for unit doors exist for exactly this situation.

The work that counts on the day is alignment. Lock body, strike and closer all have to agree, so the door latches on its own swing without being pushed or pulled to. Mention a closer when you enquire, because it changes which models are worth considering.

## Joining a Building That Already Has a Key System

Check whether the building runs a master key or an access-control system before you settle on a model. Kensington has commercial and health-care premises where every door answers to one, and adding a smart lock there is a keying question as much as a hardware one.

Where a smart lock keeps a mechanical key cylinder, that cylinder is the part that stays on the building's system, so the door does not drop off the master. Where a model has no cylinder, the door runs on its own codes, prints or app credentials, and whoever manages access needs to know beforehand. Rekeying and key systems are ordinary work for us, so say what the site already runs when you enquire and the quote accounts for it.

## Will It Go On the Door You Have Now?

Most of the answer is already on the door, in the lockset fitted to it. A smart deadbolt drops into the bore that is already there, provided the bore diameter, the backset and the door thickness suit the new lock. Where the door carries a mortice lock instead, a smart mortice lock takes over the same pocket in the door edge. Lockwood, Lane and Lemaar account for a lot of the hardware on Melbourne entries, and each makes both types.

Doors that have never carried a deadbolt get prepared from scratch: cutting and drilling to suit the lock, a longer visit than a swap and a routine one. Material and thickness decide how, so a solid timber leaf and a metal-framed entry are different jobs.

The visit finishes with the handover. Codes go in, prints are enrolled, the app is paired where the lock has one, and you work the door yourself before we pack up.

## Fitting a Lock You Bought Yourself

Buying the lock first is fine. A lock you supplied is fitted the same way as one we supply. Name the model when you enquire, and you will know whether it suits your door before a visit goes in the diary.

If you have not bought yet, four checks save the most trouble in a Kensington building:

- Whether the model comes in a fire-rated version, if the entry is a unit or common-property door.
- Whether it keeps a mechanical key cylinder, which is what lets the door stay on a building master key.
- The door's thickness, and whether a deadbolt bore already exists and where it sits.
- Whether the closer on the door will still shut it properly with the new hardware on.

## What You Might Still Want to Know

### Can I put a smart lock on my apartment door without asking anyone?

If the door is common property or a common entrance, no: the owners corporation authorises hardware changes on it. If you rent, the managing agent signs off. The quote is free either way, so it is worth having one in hand for whoever decides.

### How difficult is it to install a smart lock?

On a plain timber door with a deadbolt already in the right place, it is a straightforward swap. The part that is hard to undo comes first: reading the door, preparing it accurately, and setting lock and strike so it closes cleanly afterwards. A closer or a fire rating raises the stakes on getting that first part right.

### What are the downsides of smart locks?

Three honest ones. Batteries need changing, though the lock warns you well before it runs down. App and remote features depend on your home network reaching the door. And a mechanical key fallback has to be chosen at purchase, because a cylinder cannot be added afterwards.
