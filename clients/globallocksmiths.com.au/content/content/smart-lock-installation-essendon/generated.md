## One Feature That Cannot Be Added Later

If you want a metal key to keep opening that door, the model has to arrive with a keyway in it. Nothing can be routed in later. So the first question is not which brand, but whether a key stays in the picture at all.

Both answers are reasonable, and they feel different to live with.

- Keep the keyway. The lock carries a keyed cylinder alongside the keypad, reader or app. Anyone already holding a key gets in without learning anything new, and there is something to fall back on if the low-battery warnings go unnoticed.
- Do without it. No keys to cut, carry or collect back in. Entry runs entirely on what the lock stores, and the outside face of the door has no keyhole on it.

Whichever suits you, choose it before the box is opened. A model with a keyway can always have the key left in a drawer. A model without one cannot go the other way.

## What Has to Line Up on the Door

Your door has the final say on which models are possible at all. Three things settle that, and none of them can be judged down the phone.

- What is cut into it now. A round hole through the face means a deadbolt, a long pocket in the edge means a mortice lock, and smart versions of both exist, including fire-rated ones for unit doors. Whatever Lockwood, Lane or Lemaar hardware is there now, the new lock has to mate with the opening it leaves.
- The leaf itself. Timber and metal-framed doors are prepared differently, and every model states a thickness range rather than fitting anything at all. A door never drilled for a deadbolt is marked out and cut on site, which adds time rather than difficulty.
- Room on the outside face. A keypad or reader needs a clear run of door to sit on, so a handle set already there can decide where the new plate lands.

The visit finishes with the handover: codes loaded, any fingerprints enrolled, the app paired, and you opening the door yourself with it shut. If you kept the keyway, the key gets turned in it then rather than taken on trust.
