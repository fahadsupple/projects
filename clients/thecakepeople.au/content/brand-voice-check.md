# The Cake People — Brand Voice Compliance Check

**Date:** 13 July 2026
**Document:** `Thecakepeople.au.docx` (34 pages, ~20,600 words of body copy)
**Checked against:** The Cake People brand voice guidelines (Happy / Confident / Caring / Fun)

## Verdict: partially compliant — 2 of the 4 core adjectives land, 2 do not

The writing is genuinely good: warm, well-observed, specific, and it clearly knows the business. But it is written in a **measured, restrained, almost journalistic register**. It delivers **Confident** and **Caring** convincingly. It largely fails to deliver **Happy** and **Fun**, and it breaks the guidelines' explicit "never repetitive" rule.

This does not read like the Spice Girls, Brooki Bakehouse or Cake Mail. It reads closer to a considered feature article about a bakery.

---

## What lands ✅

**Confident** — strongly delivered. The awards (Baking Association of Australia's Victorian Baking Show, "a medal for three years running"), the 8,481 orders, "judged beside conventional egg-and-butter bakeries with no separate class for plant-based work". This is exactly the "award winners and experts in the field" authority the guidelines ask for.

**Caring** — strongly delivered, and the best thing in the document. The allergy writing is outstanding: *"No guest is handed a different cake."* / *"For a serious allergy, message us before ordering and we will be straight about what we can guarantee."* That is genuine concern for the customer, not a claim of it.

**Not aggressive or pushy** — passes cleanly. CTAs are gentle throughout ("Put your order in", "Tell us what you need"). Nothing overbearing.

---

## What does not land ❌

### 1. The register is formal — there is not a single verb contraction in 20,600 words

| Formal form used | Count | Casual form | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| "it is" | 57 | "it's" | **0** |
| "you are" | 24 | "you're" | **0** |
| "that is" | 21 | "that's" | **0** |
| "do not" | 2 | "don't" | 1 |

The only apostrophes in the document are possessives ("child's", "Australia's"). Every single verb contraction has been avoided. This is the biggest single reason the copy reads distant rather than fun and friendly — nobody speaks this way. Brooki Bakehouse's "simplified, approachable style" is built on contractions.

**This one fix would change the feel of the whole document more than anything else on this list.**

### 2. The happy/fun vocabulary is essentially absent

Across 20,600 words:

- joy: **0** · delight: **0** · magic: **0** · celebrate: **0** · smile: **0** · excited: **0**
- happy: **2** · fun: **2** · love: **2**
- Exclamation marks: **6** in the entire document (and most sit inside quoted customer reviews, not in our own voice)

For a brand whose top adjective is "Happy" and whose tone should be "cheerful and uplifting, spreading joy with every message", the emotional lexicon simply is not there. The copy describes cakes accurately; it does not sound delighted by them.

There is **no cheekiness anywhere** in the document, despite "Our tone is often cheeky" being an explicit guideline.

### 3. It is repetitive — which the guidelines explicitly forbid

The guidelines say the voice is "never... repetitive". The document uses the customer-review formula **"As one customer put it…" / "As one parent wrote…" 27 times across 34 pages**, and recycles the same handful of reviews near-verbatim:

- *"A cake that tastes amazing and catered to..."* — used on ~6 pages
- *"They kept great communication with my order status..."* — used on ~5 pages
- *"the cake was absolutely delicious and everyone..."* — used on ~5 pages

A reader browsing three collection pages will see the same review, introduced the same way, three times. This actively undermines the "award-winning, best of the best" positioning — it reads like there are only four reviews in existence.

---

## Recommended fixes, in priority order

1. **Contractions pass across the whole document.** Convert "it is" → "it's", "you are" → "you're", "that is" → "that's" wherever it's our voice speaking (not in formal claims). Single highest-impact change.
2. **Warmth pass on openers and closers.** Every page's opening hook and closing CTA should carry some genuine delight. The Barbie opener — *"She wants Barbie, she wants pink, and she wants the doll right in the middle of it."* — is the one moment in the document that sounds like the brand. There should be 34 of those, one per page.
3. **Vary the review formula.** Cap "As one customer put it" at a handful of uses, rotate in a wider set of reviews, and vary how they're introduced. If there genuinely are only a few reviews available, use fewer of them rather than repeating them.
4. **Add cheek.** At least one playful line per page. There is currently none.

---

## Separate issue — a broken sentence on Page 1

The Barbie Cakes page contains an incomplete sentence with a missing clause and a lowercase start:

> "A doll cake has to taste as good as it looks once the gown is cut, and that side is on the record. **in the Baking Association of Australia's Victorian Baking Show**, judged beside conventional egg-and-butter bakeries..."

Something has been dropped between "on the record." and "in the Baking Association". Needs repair regardless of the voice work.
